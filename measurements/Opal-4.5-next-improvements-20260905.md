# Opal 4.5 — next eight improvements

Recommendations only. Based on the September 5 hardening verification: CPU passes at +0.156% of one core, private-commit overhead passes at +13.44 MB, working-set overhead fails at +22.78 MB against +16 MB, and the five-minute soak contains an unresolved late allocation step of roughly 99 MB (+92.50 MB net). No new product changes were made for this proposal.

1. **Identify the late memory jump.** Capture memory-type snapshots and heap/virtual-allocation stacks around the five-minute mark in matched stock and Opal runs. Establish whether Windows, an Opal component, or another loaded component owns the allocation before fixing it.
2. **Measure and reduce each component's memory cost.** Compare Shell, Media, Performance, and the full suite with the same workload and timing. Profile the largest contributor and bring full-suite working-set overhead within the existing +16-MB limit while keeping CPU within budget.
3. **Bound caches and load expensive resources on demand.** Audit artwork/decoded images, history buffers, detailed hardware providers, and retained XAML objects. Where profiling identifies avoidable retention, cap owned caches and release resources when their view/session ends. These are candidates, not established causes of the jump.
4. **Extend the soak to realistic interaction.** Run 60–120-minute matched tests with track changes, repeated panel open/close, idle periods, and controlled provider failures. Check repeatable memory steps and whether resources settle after each cycle, alongside handles/GDI/USER counts.
5. **Isolate remaining potentially blocking provider calls.** Evaluate moving media-provider acquisition and reads out of Explorer into a restartable companion, keeping taskbar UI in Explorer. Test that a hung provider leaves the taskbar responsive with a clear stale/unavailable state. This is a larger architecture change with additional IPC/lifecycle complexity.
6. **Replace sliced helper sleeps with unload-aware waits.** Remove the remaining 100-ms wake-up loops through a stop event or equivalent wait. Preserve immediate cancellation and confirmed worker exit, then measure whether wake-ups/CPU improve; these loops are not a demonstrated dominant cost.
7. **Automate cold-start and display recovery stress.** Exercise repeated cold Explorer starts, delayed taskbar loading, widget toggles, and supported display reconnect/sleep transitions. Require current attachment proof on the intended display, accurate health, and preserved settings after recovery.
8. **Add bounded diagnostic and responsiveness metrics.** Record attachment duration, telemetry age, provider timeouts, restart reason, and panel-open latency in small rotating local logs. Correlate memory steps with component events and establish median/tail latency baselines without introducing per-sample disk writes.

Start with 1 and 2. Their evidence should determine the cache/lifecycle edits in 3. Keep the working-set gate failed until a current, valid measurement meets it.

Sources: [local verification](Opal-4.5-hardening-verification-20260905.md), [Microsoft VMMap](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sysinternals/downloads/vmmap), [Microsoft WPR heap analysis](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/test/wpt/recording-for-heap-analysis). Tool documentation confirms profiling capability, not allocation attribution or guaranteed savings.
